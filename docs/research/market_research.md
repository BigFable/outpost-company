# Market Research: Idle Roguelite Skilling Game ("idle roguelite RuneScape")

**As of:** 22 October 2026. All prices are US Steam list prices on the date checked. Review counts change daily. Estimates from third-party trackers (Raijin, SteamSpy) are **modelled guesses, not reported financials**, and are marked "(est.)".

---

## Executive summary

- **There is a proven audience, and it is willing to pay.** Melvor Idle, a skilling idle game inspired by RuneScape and started by one developer, sells for $9.99 plus three $4.99 expansions. It has about 92% positive reviews on Steam, more than 1 million players across platforms (claimed by the publisher in 2021), and an estimated $3.4M gross on Steam (Raijin est.). Jagex picked it up as publisher. Jagex also licensed the RuneScape *name* to WalkScape for marketing. **I could not verify the "Games Workshop" deal in the brief.** Every source ties Melvor to Jagex Partners (2021), not Games Workshop.
- **The "idle + roguelite" space is crowded on the action side and thin on the skilling side.** Survivor-style idle roguelites are common (Limitless Survivor, Nomad Idle, Dwarf Eats Mountain). Melvor's Ancient Relics mode adds random relics to skilling but is not a run-based perk draft. The opening is **"Melvor-style skilling, where each skill's prestige is a Balatro-style draft run."** It is a clear position but not a guaranteed one.
- **The idle niche on Steam is growing fast and cheap.** About 1,566 idler games had released on Steam in 2026 by late September, roughly 150% more than the same weeks of 2025. The median paid idler costs $3.99. Only about 5.6% of idlers reach 1,000 reviews (roughly $150K gross by rule of thumb). Games priced at $10–14.99 did best in that sample, but the sample is small.
- **Recommended model: a premium Steam game ($5.99–$9.99) with an optional $3–5 supporter pack**, then paid expansions later. This matches Melvor ($9.99 + DLC), Rusty's Retirement ($7 + $4 supporter pack, about 11% bought it), and Gnorp ($6.99). Free-to-play with in-app purchases (IdleOn, NGU, The Tower) works, but it needs live-ops and economy design that one developer is unlikely to sustain.
- **Wishlists still predict launch sales.** The median game sells about 0.10–0.15× its launch wishlists in week one (GameDiscoverCo, Nov 2025). The median Next Fest gain was about 950 wishlists (Oct 2024 survey). Games entering Next Fest with fewer than about 2,000 wishlists rarely break out.
- **Idle games are fun to watch, and YouTube and streamers drive their breakouts.** Gnorp launched with only about 1.7K wishlists and sold more than 120K copies in a month, mostly through YouTube. Rusty's Retirement launched with about 140K wishlists after social clips and streamers spread it. Design a mini-game and prestige moment that look good in a clip.
- **Do not put "RuneScape" or "Melvor" in the title, capsule art, or tags in a way that suggests endorsement.** Jagex's fan content policy prohibits standalone games built on its IP. A plain-text comparison such as "for fans of skilling idle games like…" is generally lower risk, but get a lawyer's view before you rely on it. Jagex has licensed its name to one similar game (WalkScape), so a licence is possible later.
- **Steam AI disclosure (rewritten January 2026):** you must disclose AI-generated content that players see in the game or on the store page, such as art, text, or audio. AI coding assistants are explicitly exempt. If you use AI art, plan for the disclosure label.
- **Realistic path (my estimate, not sourced):** vertical slice in about 2–3 months, Steam page and demo in about 5–7 months, Early Access in about 10–14 months. Cash budget is roughly $1.5K–$8K, mostly for capsule art, a trailer, and an LLC.

## 2. Is idle + roguelite already done?

- **Survivor-style idle roguelites are now common.** Examples: Limitless Survivor (July 2026; reviewers mixed, citing shallow builds), Nomad Idle (2025, auto-battling with build choices), and Dwarf Eats Mountain ($9.99, May 2026, 81% of 1.2K reviews positive, tagged roguelite, with "faster each run" scaling).
- **Skilling idlers are well established but rarely run-based.** Melvor, Idle Clans, Idle Skilling, and IdleOn all use long persistent progression. Melvor's Ancient Relics mode adds random relic drops per skill, which is the closest existing idea, but it is a separate mode, not a draft run.
- **Gap and positioning:** No source I found combines (a) skilling in the RuneScape/Melvor style, (b) **per-skill** prestige played as a short draft run with rule-changing perks, and (c) **active mini-games** that pay out more than idling. Pitch it as *"Melvor's skilling meets Balatro's runs."* It needs proof: the Limitless Survivor reviews show players punish thin builds and prestige resets that take a lot and give back little. Perks must change the rules, not just add percentages.

## 3. Platforms and business models

| Option | Evidence | Fit for solo developer |
|---|---|---|
| **Steam premium + supporter pack + paid DLC** | Melvor ($9.99 + $4.99 DLC). Rusty's $4 pack bought by about 11% of players (about $111K). In the steampageanalyzer sample, the $10–14.99 band had the best rate of reaching 1,000 reviews (only 31 games) | **Best fit** |
| **Steam free-to-play + in-app purchases/DLC** | IdleOn, NGU, Leaf Blower. 26% of idlers are free-to-play, compared with 18% of all Steam games | Needs economy tuning and live-ops. High review risk (IdleOn is 75% positive) |
| **Mobile free-to-play (ads + in-app purchases)** | The Tower: price tiers from $4.99 to $99.99 plus a $9.99 ad removal. Egg Inc: optional rewarded ads plus currency packs | High user-acquisition cost. Better as a later port |
| **Mobile free to try + one-time unlock** | Melvor's mobile model | A good later port. Apple takes 15% under its Small Business Program (under $1M). Google's fees are changing in 2026 (20–25% + 5% billing in the US for new installs) |
| **Web (itch.io / own site)** | itch.io lets you choose its cut (10% by default) and can act as merchant of record that handles VAT | Free demo, playtest funnel, and community building. Not a main revenue source |

**Cross-save:** Melvor's cloud save across platforms is part of its pitch. A browser-based (HTML/JS) build makes ports through Electron, Capacitor, or similar wrappers fairly cheap. Plan the save format early.

**Price points:** the median idler is $3.99. Comparable solo hits sell for $2.99–$9.99. Melvor is $9.99 with $4.99 expansions.

## 4. Steam launch mechanics

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Steam Direct fee | $100 per app. Recouped once the game reaches $1,000 adjusted gross revenue |
| Revenue share | 30% → 25% after $10M → 20% after $50M |
| Coming Soon page | At least 2 weeks before release. Submit for review at least 7 business days before you want it live. Going public early is fine if the design is stable |
| Next Fest | Once per game, before release. The demo must be live, with the store page public and a trailer. Submit the demo build 3–5 weeks ahead. Upcoming: 22 Feb–1 Mar 2027, 14–21 Jun 2027 (October 2026 ran 19–26 Oct) |
| Next Fest benchmarks | Oct 2024 survey: median 944 wishlists, 70th percentile 1,864. Day-1 wishlists × about 6.7 ≈ festival total. About 1 in 5 demo players wishlisted. Feb 2025: games starting with 1–10K wishlists gained a median of 1,513 (+45%). Suggested entry minimum is about 2K |
| Wishlist → sales | Week-one sales are a median of 0.15× wishlists for games with 25K+ wishlists, and 0.10× for games priced above $10. Results vary tenfold between games. Underperformers spent longer in pre-release (median 411 days vs 214) |
| Demo | Release it *before* Next Fest and test it with streamers first (Zukowski, via Unity blog) |
| Playtest | Steam Playtest is free and separate from the main game. It does not affect reviews or wishlists |
| Early Access rules | Must be playable. No promised dates or features. The Steam price cannot be higher than elsewhere. No permanent discounts. Raising the price blocks discounts for 30 days. Once released, a game cannot return to Early Access |

**Early Access norms for idle games:** Melvor spent about 2 years in Early Access ($9.99 at 1.0), Increlution has been in Early Access since 2021, and Idle Clans launched as Early Access. Idle players expect updates and accept Early Access. Hades sold 700K copies during Early Access.

## 5. Marketing channels

- **r/incremental_games:** posts must link to playable content, with at most one new post per game every 30 days. Between posts, use the weekly Feedback Friday thread. Games built around real money or crypto are banned. Developers routinely post browser prototypes, itch.io playtests, and Steam demos there, so your HTML/JS prototype fits well.
- **YouTube and streamers:** these drove Gnorp's sales, with an unusually low 1.7K launch wishlists. Big "number go up" upgrade moments make good videos. Rusty's Retirement let Twitch viewers spawn on the farm.
- **Short-form social (TikTok, Reels, X):** Rusty's grew from clips of its "sits at the bottom of your screen" hook.
- **Discord:** Melvor and IdleOn communities centre on Discord and wikis. Use it for a feedback loop on balance and perk design.
- **Steam event timing:** Rusty launched just before a themed fest and got two sales peaks. A relevant upcoming event is the Shop Keeper Fest (25 Jan–1 Feb 2027).
- **Paid options, if any:** capsule art from about $250. Trailer from $500–1K (newcomer editor) to $3–6K (professional). Ads cost about $1–2 per wishlist. Spending more than $10K has thin evidence behind it.

## 6. Legal and practical

- **Trademarks:** Infringement depends on whether customers are likely to be confused about who made or sponsors the product. US law allows "nominative" use of another brand's name to *refer to* that product, as in comparative advertising. In practice:
  - Use your own name and art. Keep "RuneScape" and "Melvor" out of the title, capsule art, and logo.
  - At most, include one plain-text line in the description, for example "Inspired by classic skilling games like RuneScape and Melvor Idle." Add no Jagex logos and nothing that implies endorsement.
  - Do not copy Jagex assets, names, items, or lore. Jagex's fan content policy bans fan *games* and limits sales of fan content to small runs.
  - For a licensed route, look to WalkScape: Jagex let it use the RuneScape name in marketing only.
  - This is not legal advice. Have a trademark attorney check the store copy.
- **LLC (US):** by default a single-member LLC is a "disregarded entity." Profit goes on your personal return (Schedule C) and is subject to self-employment tax. An EIN is needed for employees and some other cases, and banks often ask for one. The LLC mainly gives liability separation. State filing fees vary, and I did not check them.
- **Taxes and payments:** Steam collects and remits VAT in listed countries and sales tax in some US and Canadian jurisdictions. US developers submit a W-9. A single-member LLC receives a 1099, while an LLC taxed as a C or S corporation does not. itch.io can act as merchant of record if you use its Payouts option.
- **AI assets and code (Steam, January 2026 rewrite):**
  - You must disclose AI-*generated content* in the game or on the store page or marketing, and describe the tools in a text box.
  - You must also disclose any AI content generated live during play.
  - AI coding and workflow tools are *not* covered.
  - Some buyers react badly to the AI disclosure label. Using human-made or bought art for the capsule and key art reduces that risk.

## Recommendations

1. **Platform:** Steam (PC/Mac/Linux, Steam Deck friendly) first. Use a free web or itch.io build as a playtest and community funnel. Port to mobile later as free-to-try with a one-time unlock, following Melvor.
2. **Model:** Premium, priced at **$6.99–$9.99** at Early Access launch (a lower launch price is fine, raised toward 1.0). Add a **$3–5 cosmetic supporter pack**. Plan $4.99 expansions after 1.0. Avoid free-to-play with in-app purchases unless you want to run live-ops.
3. **Setting:** Choose the one that gives the most distinctive capsule art and the most distinctive perk "rule changes." A historical frontier or industrial setting stands out more from Melvor and Idle Clans, which are fantasy. Fantasy is a safer match for the RuneScape audience. (This is judgement, not sourced.)
4. **Prove the hook early:** make the per-skill draft run the core of the demo. Perks should break rules, for example "trees fall in chains" or "axes duplicate drops," not just give +10%. Make sure the run is short enough to clip.
5. **Launch path and milestones:**
   - Month 0–3: vertical slice, then post the web build to Feedback Friday.
   - Month 4–6: Steam page live, Discord, devlogs and short clips, outreach to idle-game YouTubers.
   - Month 6–8: public demo out before Next Fest, plus Steam Playtest.
   - Next Fest in June 2027 or October 2027, with 2K+ wishlists.
   - Early Access about 1–3 months after Next Fest, timed next to a relevant themed fest.
   - Full release after 9–18 months of Early Access.
6. **Legal hygiene:**
   - Form a US single-member LLC before you take revenue.
   - Use an original name and art.
   - Mention RuneScape or Melvor only in descriptive copy, if at all, and have a lawyer check it.
   - Fill in Steam's AI disclosure for any AI-generated content players will see.
