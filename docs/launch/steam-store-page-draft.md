## 1. Short description

Steam shows this under the trailer and in search. Draft (243 characters; I did not verify Steam's character limit, so check it when entering):

> 1890. A chartered company lands on a fog-covered coast. Every skill is a run: fell, saw and build an outpost with drafted perks that change the rules, then close camp and load the ship before winter. Idle always works. Active play pays faster.

Shorter option for the capsule-sized blurb (about 120 characters):

> Idle skilling where every skill is a roguelite run. Draft perks, build an outpost, close camp before winter.

## 2. Long description

> **Every skill is a run. Every run is different.**
>
> It is 1890. The Outpost Company has a royal charter, a ship, and a fog-covered coast nobody has mapped. You are the outpost manager. Set your crews to work and they keep working, even while you are away. Take the wheel yourself and they work faster.
>
> **Start with the axe.**
> Logging is your first skill. Pick a tract, hire crews, buy better tools, and watch timber move from stump to landing to buyer. Then play the four hands-on jobs when you want to push: place a fall so the trees tip like dominoes, plan the cuts on a stem, skid a turn through the mud, drive a loaded sledge down a winding road. None of them is required. All of them pay.
>
> **Draft your run.**
> Each outpost is a roguelite run. Between stages you are dealt perk cards, and the good ones change the rules instead of adding a percentage. Pick a Domino-fall build, a Prime-timber build, or something stranger. When winter nears, close camp: strip the outpost, load the charter ship, and bank Charter Shares that make the next run start stronger.
>
> **One company, many camps.**
> Close camp on Logging and the company opens a Sawmill. Logs from one camp feed the other. Camp structures stay built, so each camp gets easier and your choices get more interesting. Closing one camp never stops the others.
>
> **The coast has a mind of its own.**
> Every season the weather deals a card: a thaw flood, an equinox gale, a blizzard that closes the woods. Each bad day has a silver lining and a way through if you are watching. Each tree species has its own strengths: spruce for spars, oak for barrels, giant cloudwood for the city's beams. [check: species and weather are in the build for playtest round 3]
>
> **Built for the long haul.**
> Offline progress, so the camp keeps working while you are gone. [check] Steam Cloud saves. [check] No combat, no timers that punish you, no microtransactions.
>
> **Early Access.**
> Outpost Company is in Early Access at $7.99 for about six months, and the price rises to $9.99 at 1.0. [check: owner confirms wording and that this is allowed by Steam's Early Access rules, which bar promising dates or features] Early Access includes Logging and the Sawmill with more camps on the way. We plan at least three years of free updates after 1.0, with no paid expansions.

*Writing notes:* the "dominoes" line is the strongest clip-able idea, so it appears twice. The Early Access paragraph must not promise dates or features; Steam's Early Access rules bar this, so the master dev should check the paragraph against the current rules before it goes up.

## 3. Feature bullets (5 to 7)

1. **Every skill is a roguelite run.** Draft perks, build an outpost, close camp. The next run starts stronger.
2. **Perks that change the rules.** Trees fall in chains. Prime logs unlock charter orders. A flat +10% is never the reward.
3. **Idle is always enough.** Your crews work while you are away. You can close camp every run without touching a mini-game.
4. **Hands-on mini-games that pay more.** Logging has four (place a fall, plan the cuts, skid a turn, drive a load). All are optional, and active players move faster than idle ones.
5. **Two camps that feed each other.** Logs to the Sawmill, planks back to the logging camp, with their own cash and their own runs. [check: Fishing and others are not promised in this list]
6. **Weather and species.** A new weather card every season, six tree species that each play differently.
7. **No combat. Fog, ink and ledgers.** An 1890 alt-history frontier with a gentle, readable look.

## 5. Capsule art brief (for a human artist)

**Sizes (Steam's graphical assets page):** main capsule 1232 x 706 px, header 920 x 430 px, small 462 x 174 px, vertical 748 x 896 px. Screenshots at least 1920 x 1080, 16:9, at least 5. **Header, main and vertical capsules may contain no text except the game title.** The small capsule needs the logo to nearly fill it. No awards, quotes or marketing lines in the art.

**What the main capsule should show.** A foggy 1890 coast at the edge of morning. In the middle ground, a line of tall pines tipping over like dominoes, each one nudging the next, with the first tree just starting to go. That image is the game's hook and it is easy to read at a glance. Behind the trees, the fog swallows the ridge. At the lower left, a small company camp: a canvas tent, a lantern, stacked planks. At the far right, in the fog, the silhouette of a charter ship with its lamps lit. A tiny figure with an axe can stand in front for scale, but the trees are the hero. No combat, no weapons, no monsters.

**Mood and palette.** Calm, a little eerie, handsomely old-fashioned. Think engraving and trade-ledger illustration, in cool greens and grey fog with one warm accent (lantern and the orange the game already uses, `#E0560B`). The game's current palette is mossy greens (`#2E5C39`), bark brown (`#6B4A2F`) and paper greys. Match it so the capsule looks like the game.

**Title treatment.** "Outpost Company" in a slab-serif or engraved lettering (the game uses Zilla Slab). Legible at 184 x 69 px.

**Vertical capsule.** Same trees, tipped toward the title; the ship stays small near the bottom.

**What not to do.** Do not include "RuneScape" or "Melvor", or any art, fonts, icons or colors that imitate their logos. No Jagex-style shields, skill icons or the scroll look. Do not show anything the game does not have.

**AI rules and trust. Please read before commissioning.**
- Steam's Content Survey asks you to disclose AI-generated content that ships with the game and that players consume: artwork, sound, narrative, localization. If you use AI services you must describe how. Valve reviews AI content like any other and checks it matches your marketing. AI tools used only for development efficiency, such as code assistants, are not the focus (Steam's content survey page; VGC reported the January 2026 rewrite).
- The 23 Oct market watch found that Next Fest had 7,200 to 8,000 demos, more than 1,000 with an AI disclosure, and that press coverage is dominated by "AI slop" complaints. A capsule with an AI label is a risk at the one moment buyers decide in under ten seconds.
- **Recommendation:** commission a human artist, put "no generative AI in the work or the process" in the brief, and ask for the layered source file. Budget $250 to $1,000 (market research estimate).
- **Open question for the owner, and I need your answer before the page goes up:** much of the game's text (item names, perk descriptions, dispatches, story cards) was drafted by Claude during development. Steam's survey names narrative and localization as covered. Valve's page says AI used for development efficiency is not the focus, but I cannot say where the line falls for written game text. **Please ask a lawyer or Valve's support before you answer the survey.** If it must be disclosed, say so honestly on the page. This store text was also drafted by AI. Rewrite it in your own voice before posting.
- Music and sound: buy licensed audio from human composers; do not use AI-generated music.

## 6. "Inspired by" wording (flag for a lawyer)

This is not legal advice. Jagex's fan content policy bars standalone fan games built on its IP, and Jagex has licensed its name once, to WalkScape, for marketing only. Melvor Idle is published by Jagex. Melvor's own Steam page says it is "inspired by RuneScape", but that is Jagex's own page and says nothing about what a third party may say.

| Option | Wording | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. No names (recommended default)** | "For fans of long skilling idle games, with a roguelite twist." Rely on tags (Idler, Incremental) and "More Like This" to find the audience | Lowest. Costs some search discoverability |
| **B. One plain line, no logos** | "Inspired by classic skilling idle games." No game names, only a genre | Low |
| **C. Name the games once, in plain text** | "If you like skilling idle games like Melvor Idle, you will feel at home." No logos, no implied endorsement, nowhere else on the page | Moderate. Comparison by name is generally lower risk in US law than using a mark as a brand, but a lawyer should confirm before use |

Rules for all options: never put either name in the title, the capsule, the trailer, the logo, tags or the store URL. Do not copy skill names or icons. Keep the same discipline in posts on Reddit and Discord: plain descriptive text only. I do not decide this. Recommend asking a trademark lawyer to review sections 1, 2 and 6 and the name "Outpost Company" in one session, before the page is submitted.

## 7. Trailer shot list (30 to 45 seconds)

Steam's trailer guidance: lead with gameplay from the player's point of view, keep the HUD visible, assume viewers watch muted, you have under ten seconds to hook someone. 1920 x 1080, 30 or 60 fps, .mp4. Steam also builds a six-second looping micro-trailer from your first video, so the first six seconds must work alone.

| Time | Shot | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 to 0:06 | **Domino fall.** One tree placed, then the line goes down in a chain, numbers popping and the cash counter spinning up | The hook and the micro-trailer. Pure "number go up" |
| 0:06 to 0:12 | **A draft.** Three perk cards slide in; the player picks the one that changes the rules, and the next fall changes with it. Caption on the card text itself | Shows "perks change rules" in 6 seconds |
| 0:12 to 0:18 | **Fast cuts, 1.5 s each:** plan the cuts, skid a turn, drive a load | Shows there is more than one thing to do |
| 0:18 to 0:26 | **Blizzard card.** Season dispatch slides in; the woods whiten, the road edges go dashed; the player counters with a good run and the card says "packed roads" | The weather is the freshest clip; it shows a bad event with a way through |
| 0:26 to 0:33 | **Opening the Sawmill.** The directors' note: "The company will no longer ship raw logs home. Saw them here." The tab opens, the saw pit runs, boards stack | The camp-to-camp payoff |
| 0:33 to 0:41 | **Closing camp.** The outpost strips down, the charter ship loads at dusk, Charter Shares count up, and the next run starts already faster | The emotional end of a run |
| 0:41 to 0:45 | Title card: **Outpost Company**. Tagline "Every skill is a run. Every run is different." Wishlist now | Call to action |

Sound: licensed human-made music and sound effects; the trailer must make sense with sound off. No voiceover. Do not show Reddit, Discord or review quotes. Record the real game so the trailer matches what buyers get; if it is not there yet, record when it is. **Capture every playtest session and keep any clip with a big number or a clean chain** for devlogs.

If the trailer is not ready when the page is submitted, the page can go up with screenshots and add the trailer later; Steam's own page says a trailer is "ideal", not mandatory. Expect fewer wishlists without one.
