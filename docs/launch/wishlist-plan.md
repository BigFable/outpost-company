# Wishlist plan: 25 Oct 2026 to Next Fest, June 2027

*Draft by the launch agent, 25 Oct 2026, for the owner to approve. Nothing here is posted, emailed or sent. Dates come from Steam's festival schedule and `docs/service-plan.md` (gates G3 to G5). Wishlist targets and hours are my planning estimates, not sourced; the benchmarks they are checked against are cited at the end.*

## The short version

- **Goal:** at least **2,000 wishlists by 10 May 2027**, the day we must submit the demo for Next Fest (14 to 21 Jun 2027). Under 2,000, we wait for the October 2027 Next Fest instead (dates not yet announced in the schedule I opened).
- **Steam page goes live around 12 Jan 2027** (submit for review by 14 Dec).
- **Owner time:** about 3 to 5 hours a week for marketing on top of game work, rising to 8 to 10 in Next Fest weeks. This sits inside the service plan's 4 to 6 hours per week plus release-week peaks. If it does not fit, cut devlogs to every second week first.
- **Honest warning:** 2,000 wishlists from zero in about 4 months with no audience is possible but not likely without a clip that spreads. The benchmark article I opened puts the launch floor at around 7,000 wishlists (for Steam's Popular Upcoming list). Early Access at $7.99 with 3,000 to 4,000 wishlists would be a modest launch, not a failure, but plan for it.

## Timeline

Weekly effort is the owner's time on marketing only. "Agents" means the launch agent drafts, the owner approves.

### Phase 0. Foundations (26 Oct to 22 Nov 2026), 2 to 3 hours a week
- **This week:** owner creates the itch.io account and uploads the playtest (see `itch-playtest-guide.md`). Private only.
- Keep screen recordings of every playtest session. Save any clip with a big number, a Domino chain or a blizzard. These become devlog and trailer material.
- **By 9 Nov:** owner books one session with a trademark lawyer to review the name, the store text and the "inspired by" line (`steam-store-page-draft.md`, section 6).
- **By 16 Nov:** owner decides on spend: $100 Steam Direct fee, capsule art ($250 to $1,000). Commission the capsule from a human artist. Allow 3 to 4 weeks.
- Decide the AI-disclosure question for in-game text (store draft, section 5).
- Start building the creator list privately (see "Creator outreach", below). No contact.

### Phase 1. Page build (23 Nov to 11 Jan), 3 to 4 hours a week
- Capsule art delivered by about 11 Dec. Choose 5 or more screenshots.
- Trailer: record after the vertical slice, mid-January. If it is not ready, submit without it.
- **14 Dec:** mark the store page "Ready for Review" (Steam wants 7 business days before you want it live; the holidays make this tight, so I added slack).
- Prepare the first devlog and the Discord server (empty, private) during this time.
- **Winter Sale (17 Dec to 4 Jan)** means more people on Steam. The page won't be live yet, but that is fine: do not rush.

### Phase 2. Page live (12 Jan to 21 Feb), 4 to 5 hours a week
- **~12 Jan:** click "Post as Coming Soon" once the owner approves. Same day: open the Discord to the public, post devlog #1 on the Steam page's community hub, and one post on Feedback Friday with the free itch.io web build (needs the owner to switch the itch page to Public, or a separate public build).
- **Cadence:** one short devlog every two weeks on Steam, each with a clip. One Discord update a week. One short video a week (10 to 20 seconds, no voiceover needed) on whatever short-video platform the owner prefers. Reuse devlog clips.
- Wishlist target by 1 Feb: ~300. By 21 Feb: ~600 (planning numbers).
- **Watch, don't enter, Next Fest 22 Feb to 1 Mar.** Play the idle demos; note which clip, tag and capsule choices got traction.
- Themed festivals nearby (Shop Keeper Fest 25 Jan to 1 Feb; Desktop Companion Fest 14 to 18 Jan) do not match an idle skilling game. The schedule I opened lists no idle, incremental, roguelite or city-builder fest, so skip them.

### Phase 3. Demo and creators (22 Feb to 9 May), 5 to 6 hours a week
- **By 1 Apr:** demo build (web) on itch.io, public. Then the one big r/incremental_games post (below).
- **Early April:** with the owner's approval, send the first batch of key offers to the creators on the list. Keep batches small (5 to 10 a time) so replies can be handled.
- Wishlist targets (planning numbers): 1,000 by 1 Apr; 1,500 by 25 Apr; **2,000 by 10 May**.
- Collect demo feedback in Discord; fix the top three annoyances each week.
- Use Steam Playtest (free; does not affect reviews or wishlists) if more testers are needed.

### Phase 4. Next Fest prep (10 May to 13 Jun), 6 to 8 hours a week
- **10 May:** run G5. If the count is 2,000 or more, **submit the Steam demo build now**: Steam asks for 5 weeks before the fest if you want Press Preview and 3 weeks (24 May) if the demo goes live before the fest. Registration closes on a date I could not find, so check the Next Fest page in Steamworks in April.
- Release the demo a few days before the fest starts so streamers can play it. Steam suggests at least 30 minutes before; earlier is safer.
- Tell the list the date. Email the creator list a reminder (owner approves each).

### Phase 5. Next Fest (14 to 21 Jun 2027), 8 to 10 hours that week
- Be on Discord daily. Post one clip a day. Reply to every demo review that a person bothered to write.
- Do not announce an Early Access date in the middle of Next Fest; Steam's rules bar promised dates.
- **After:** the Summer Sale runs 24 Jun to 8 Jul. We will have no game to discount, so use the time to write the post-fest report and plan Early Access (about Sep 2027 per the service plan).

## Rules and cadence for r/incremental_games

I could not open the subreddit's rules page (blocked), so this is from the mirror of the subreddit I opened and from the 22 Oct market research. **Re-read the sidebar before every post.**

- A new post per game is limited to **one every 30 days (Rule 1D) and must link to playable content.** Spend it on the public web demo, not on a Steam page announcement.
- Feedback Friday is the weekly thread for unfinished games. Use it every second Friday from Jan, with a short note on what changed.
- The recommendation thread discourages posting your own game.
- Giveaways need moderator approval. Games built around real money or crypto are banned.
- The page did not mention AI. Read the current rules before posting anything with AI-made assets.
- The owner posts, not an agent. Draft is OK; the post stays human-written.

Planned posts: 1 Feedback Friday (about 15 Jan), then the demo post (about 2 to 10 Apr), then a Next Fest reminder in the Friday thread (11 Jun). Stay 30 days apart.

## Discord

- Create the server in December, private. Open ~12 Jan.
- Channels: announcements, feedback, bugs, clips, balance ideas. Keep it small. Melvor's and IdleOn's communities centre on Discord (market research).
- Owner time: 1 to 2 hours a week. Ask players for balance and perk feedback, which is what agents use for the next tuning pass.
- Moderation: set two simple rules (be kind; no spoilers in announcements) and name one friend as a backup moderator.

## Creator outreach: criteria only (no contacting)

The launch agent builds the list; the owner approves every message before it is sent. Criteria:

1. Has posted at least two idle, incremental or "number go up" videos in the last 60 days.
2. Audience large enough to matter but small enough to reply: roughly 5,000 to 300,000 subscribers or followers (my judgement).
3. Plays unreleased games and demos on camera. Skip anyone whose channel says they never accept keys.
4. Plays games the way ours plays: long-run skilling or roguelite idlers, not shooters.
5. A public business email or contact form. Never DM through a private channel.
6. No history of paid-only coverage that must be disclosed in a way we cannot afford.
7. Look for who covered Gnorp and Rusty's Retirement, both of which broke out through YouTube (market research), and who covered the October 2026 idle demos.

List 30 to 50 candidates by February. Send in batches of 5 to 10 from April.
